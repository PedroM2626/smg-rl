# One additional hour per Sweet Sweet reward

**Correction:** The previously reported first-segment projection is withdrawn as
an invalid measure of visited progress. See [the investigation](progress-metric-interpretation-20261028.md).
The original recorded values remain in immutable audit artifacts only.

Owner-authorized October 28 continuation. This is a new finite study following
the completed inconclusive short A/B. It does not overwrite or retroactively
change that study's gate. One training seed (142) per reward is intentionally an
exploratory trade-off: results cannot establish statistical significance or
generalization.

## Source and schedule

The opening control and full-human-route candidate each resume their own
reviewed 6,144-real-decision seed-142 checkpoint from
`.local/human-route-ab-fixed-20261028/state.json`. Each exact resume preserves
that branch's online and target weights, optimizer, replay, counter and RNG;
the branches never exchange replay or reward data. Source artifacts and the
original 749,006-decision common ancestor remain unchanged. Both use IQN,
IMPALA, four stacked grayscale frames, eight exclusive 12-frame actions, six
workers and the same canonical-start distribution. There is no world model,
imagination or novelty bonus.

One additional hour per arm is split into two 1,800-second finite blocks. The
order is control half, candidate half, control second half, candidate second
half; one training job at a time. Every block requires a clean wall-budget
finish, reconciled real/replay/attempt counts, finite metrics and matching
checkpoint/replay signatures before its evaluation or a successor. The normal
training clock includes startup; checkpoint and cleanup can add wall time.
Each block has a 999,996-decision ceiling in addition to the time cap, and must
stop for memory, disk or owned stop markers. Such a premature stop is not an
hour and must be reviewed, never silently retried. Source and final counts are
reported separately. Equal wall time may yield unequal physical decisions or
optimizer updates; these are reported and limit direct sample-efficiency claims.

After each half-hour block, run one greedy and three stochastic frozen full-start
evaluations, action seeds 11/23/47, epsilon 0.1, at most 600 decisions per
episode. Evaluation decisions and videos are separate from training. The
original Wii savestate is identical in those action draws. Record both
training-collection and frozen outcomes without conflating them.

## Predeclared measurements

- Native full-start star, verified from game RAM, is the actual success signal.
- Strict grounded human-route progress uses the same signed schema-5 route for
  both arms; its previously observed zero floor remains an important limitation.
- The predeclared first-segment projection was later found invalid as a measure
  of visited progress and is withdrawn. Its broad tolerance could award apparent
  progress while Mario remained on the starting platform.
- Also retain alive-grounded geometric projection toward the near-star point,
  health/damage, death, timeouts, attempts, actions, real decisions and updates.
  The geometric projection is not a native mission percentage or route length.

The baseline first-segment scores were also generated by the withdrawn method
and cannot establish route progress or rank either policy.

The state and exact command ledger are `.local/reward-hour-pair-20261028/state.json`;
the supervisor alone owns all four stages. After they finish, audit real totals,
hashes, evaluation policy identity and outcomes before reporting. Never duplicate
an active/completed block, blindly retry partial data, or automatically add
another hour. Reused prior evaluations and source checkpoints remain identified.

## Owner-authorized memory diagnosis and recovery

TensorBoard's last pre-stop resource sample shows 0.388 GiB available physical
RAM at 35,328 cumulative real decisions, below the unchanged 0.5 GiB guard;
available commit was 29.43 GiB. The final status memory snapshot was taken
after workers closed and must not be mistaken for the trigger snapshot. A
signed, per-user Realtek `-background` helper was using approximately 5.39 GiB
private memory; this same helper had been implicated in an earlier documented
memory leak. Its short idle measurements declined slightly, so those samples
alone do not prove the growth mechanism or exclusive cause of the full peak.
The verified user background helper was restarted without stopping the system
audio service. Free physical RAM increased from approximately 11.05 GiB to
16.29 GiB, while the replacement helper used about 5.4 MiB private memory.

The owner explicitly requested investigation and continuation. An explicit
memory-stop audit verified the candidate's exact source checkpoint, signed
reward, optimizer/replay, native results and zero CSV decision gap. Its 29,184
new real decisions and 1,052.21 elapsed seconds including cleanup are charged
once. The control's already completed 1,812.96 seconds and its frozen
evaluations are also retained and charged; none are rerun. The original failed
state and its evidence are immutable.

The isolated recovery ledger is `.local/reward-hour-recovery-20261028/state.json`.
The first segment is a 120-second health check from the exact candidate partial
checkpoint. Subsequent segments are at most 600 seconds, with explicit
checkpoint/review and at least 6 GiB available physical RAM before each
dispatch. The learner's original memory guard remains unchanged. Time including
checkpoint/cleanup is charged after every segment, with a 20-second reserve and
no dispatch when at most 30 seconds remain. Each arm stays within its original
3,600-second allocation. Splitting into shorter segments adds environment
resets; although optimizer, replay, counters and RNG are resumed, this is not
bitwise equivalent to a single uninterrupted collector. Evaluations occur once
at the pending half/full boundaries, separately from training. A repeated
resource stop or integrity failure requires a new review; never blindly retry.

The physical-memory evidence is recorded in
[the recovery audit](assets/performance/reward-hour-memory-recovery-20261028.json).

### Checkpoint-cleanup overrun and second recovery

The recovered control's last collector segment stopped at its requested wall
boundary, but closing six emulators and writing the checkpoint took 22.72 s,
more than the 20 s shutdown reserve. Its recorded cumulative elapsed time was
3,602.72 s, exceeding the intended 3,600 s by 2.72 s. The supervisor detected
this and stopped before the control's final frozen evaluation and before any
second candidate block. This was a budgeting error, not an additional planned
training block. The final control segment itself completed cleanly: its
90,384 cumulative real decisions, 720 cumulative completed attempts, agent
and replay hashes, signed reward and zero CSV gap passed a separate audit.

The owner had requested continued recovery without repeating data. A second
once-only recovery retains the failed supervisor state and the extra 2.72 s,
performs no further control training, and evaluates the audited control
checkpoint once. It resumes the candidate only from its own previously reviewed
54,654-decision checkpoint, with 1,794.85 s already charged. The candidate's
total limit remains 3,600 s. Its new supervisor reserves 120 s, rather than
20 s, for cleanup and checkpointing and will not dispatch a segment if less
than 130 s remain. This conservative reserve can leave part of the requested
hour unused; it does not retroactively erase the control overrun. Segment
budgets remain at most 600 s. Any subsequent fault retains data and must be
reviewed before another dispatch. The new ledger is
`.local/reward-hour-recovery2-20261028/state.json`.

## Final result after recovery

Both branches were audited end-to-end, counting the original control block,
the stopped candidate block and all subsequent recovery segments exactly once.
The source checkpoints, final checkpoints and replays passed hashes and reward
signature checks; real-decision CSV coverage reconciled at every segment. No
world-model or imagined transitions were used. Native completion is always a
separate RAM event.

| Arm | Accounted time including cleanup | New real decisions | New attempts | Deaths | Nonfatal HP lost | Native stars |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Opening control | 3,602.72 s | 84,240 | 670 | 533 | 0 | 0 |
| Human-route candidate | 3,504.23 s | 93,768 | 660 | 337 | 0 | 0 |

The control exceeded the 3,600-second target by 2.72 seconds during cleanup, as
recorded above. The candidate stopped 95.77 seconds short of the hour because
the revised supervisor reserved time for cleanup. The candidate nevertheless
collected 9,528 more decisions, so an equal-duration and equal-sample claim is
not warranted. The extra recovery boundaries reset emulator episodes and make
the two histories different from uninterrupted collection.

Across the sixteen frozen evaluations, no episode collected a RAM star. The
later [landmark reanalysis](assets/performance/observed-landmark-audit-v2-20261028.json)
found no candidate proximity matches to the eight ordered human-observed
landmarks. This is neither a percentage of the mission nor proof that every
other route was unvisited; it is a conservative candidate-visit check requiring
visual review. The first-segment projection figures previously published here
are invalid and withdrawn. Straight-line goal-axis projections in the historical
audit remain geometric diagnostics, not route traversal or native completion.
The same Wii state was restored for action-randomness draws, and one training
seed cannot support a general or causal reward ranking. No additional training
was launched.

The [final machine-readable audit](assets/performance/reward-hour-pair-final-20261028.json)
retains block hashes, outcomes, per-episode scores, trajectory hashes and
